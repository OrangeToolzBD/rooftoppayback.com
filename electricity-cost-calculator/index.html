Electricity Cost Calculator

An electricity cost calculator multiplies watts × hours × days ÷ 1,000 to get kWh, then multiplies by your price. A 1,500 W space heater run 4 hours a day for 30 days uses 180 kWh, about $28.69 a month at Texas’s June 2026 average of 15.94¢ (EIA).

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Rooftop Payback

How do I calculate the cost of electricity for an appliance?

Find the appliance’s watts, estimate how long it runs, convert to kWh, and multiply by your price per kWh. The formula is: monthly cost = watts × hours per day × days per month ÷ 1,000 × price.

The default list in the calculator, at the Texas price:

Space heater, 1,500 W4 h × 30 days180$28.69
Gaming PC, 400 W3 h × 30 days36$5.74
Total216$34.43

That is $413.16 a year. The wattages are examples; use the figure on your own appliance’s label or manual.

For appliances that cycle on and off, such as refrigerators, freezers and air conditioners, count only the hours the motor or compressor actually runs, or the estimate will be too high. A plug-in energy meter gives a measured figure if you have one.

Your real price per kWh is your total bill divided by the kWh used, because it includes delivery charges, fees and taxes. Enter it in the “your own price” box to replace the state average.

What is the average electricity price in my state?

The U.S. average residential price was 18.34¢ per kWh in June 2026, up from 17.47¢ in June 2025, according to EIA’s Electric Power Monthly released August 26, 2026 (EIA Table 5.6.A). State averages vary widely.

Which appliances use the most electricity?

Air conditioning is the largest electricity use in U.S. homes. EIA’s 2020 survey data put it at 19% of household electricity, followed by space heating and water heating at 12% each, with lighting and refrigerators the next largest uses (EIA, updated December 18, 2023).

Heating and cooling appliances top the list because they draw high wattage for many hours. A 1,500 W heater for 4 hours uses as much as a 400 W computer run 15 hours. Small electronics add up too when they run all day: a 150 W device left on around the clock uses 108 kWh a month.

The calculator’s tests include a mix like that: 1,000 W for 2 hours a day plus 150 W around the clock, over 30 days, totals 168 kWh, or $33.60 at 20¢.

For context, the average U.S. home bought 10,791 kWh in 2022, about 899 kWh a month (EIA). If you want to cover your usage with solar, size a system with the solar panel calculator, or convert individual loads with the kWh calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate electricity cost?

Multiply the appliance’s watts by the hours it runs, divide by 1,000 to get kWh, and multiply by your price per kWh. A 400 W computer used 3 hours a day for 30 days uses 36 kWh; at 15.94¢, that is $5.74 a month. Add up each appliance for a total.

How much electricity should a 2,000 sq ft house use?

There is no standard by floor area, since climate, heating fuel and appliances matter more. EIA reports the average U.S. residential customer bought 10,791 kWh in 2022, with Louisiana highest at 14,774 kWh and Hawaii lowest at 6,178 kWh (EIA). Compare your 12-month total with those figures.

How many kWh of electricity does $1,000 buy?

Divide the dollars by the price per kWh. At the June 2026 U.S. average of 18.34¢, $1,000 buys about 5,453 kWh. At the Texas average of 15.94¢, it buys about 6,274 kWh, and at Hawaii’s 52.72¢, about 1,897 kWh. Your utility’s fixed charges reduce what your own bill buys.
