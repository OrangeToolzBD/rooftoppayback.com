Solar Panel Calculator: How Many Panels Do I Need?

This solar panel calculator divides your yearly electricity use by what 1 kW of panels produces in your state, then by panel wattage. With Texas defaults, 1,000 kWh a month needs a 7.9 kW system, or 20 panels of 400 W, about $17,845 before incentives. Production comes from PVGIS modelling at each state capital.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Rooftop Payback

How many solar panels do I need?

Your panel count is your yearly kWh use, divided by your state’s yearly output per kW, divided by the panel size in kW, rounded up. The formula is short, but the two inputs you supply, your usage and your panel wattage, change the answer most.

With the Texas default of 1,000 kWh a month:

  1. Yearly use: 1,000 × 12 = 12,000 kWh.
  2. System size: 12,000 ÷ 1,513 kWh per kW = 7.93 kW.
  3. Panels: 7,930 W ÷ 400 W = 19.8, rounded up to 20.
  4. Cost: 7.93 kW × $2.25 per watt = about $17,845 before incentives.

Use a full 12 months of bills if you can, since summer cooling and winter heating shift monthly use a lot. For context, the average U.S. residential customer bought 10,791 kWh in 2022, about 899 kWh a month (EIA). At that level, a Texas home needs 7.1 kW, or 18 panels of 400 W.

The result assumes a well-oriented, unshaded roof. Treat it as a starting point for comparing installer designs, which model your actual roof.

How much electricity does a solar panel produce in my state?

Each kW of panels produces from 622 to 1,841 kWh a year depending on the state, according to PVGIS estimates at the 51 state capitals (PVGIS 5.2, crystalline panels, optimal tilt, 14% system losses). Southwestern capitals lead: Santa Fe is modelled at 1,841 kWh, Phoenix at 1,805 and Carson City at 1,756.

How do panel wattage and roof space change the count?

Higher-wattage panels reduce the panel count but not the system size in kW, and it is the kW that sets production and cost. In Texas, 7.93 kW takes 23 panels at 350 W, 20 at 400 W, or 18 at 450 W.

Roof space is what limits the count. Check the panel’s dimensions on its spec sheet, then leave room for any setbacks your local codes require. If the roof cannot fit the count, higher-wattage panels or a smaller system are the options.

The main electrical panel can also limit size. Under the 120% rule, NYSERDA’s formula is busbar rating × 1.2 minus main breaker rating, which gives the largest solar breaker allowed (NYSERDA). A 100-amp busbar with a 100-amp main allows a 20-amp solar breaker. A larger system may need a panel upgrade, which the cost calculator does not include.

Should I size for 100% of my usage?

Covering 100% of yearly use is a sensible default, but not always the best target. What you are paid for surplus power usually decides it. Under full-retail net metering, output up to your annual use is worth your full rate. Under net billing, exports earn less, so a smaller system that you use mostly yourself can pay back faster per dollar.

The offset input lets you test this. In Texas, covering 80% of 12,000 kWh needs 6.35 kW and 16 panels, at about $14,276, compared with 7.93 kW and $17,845 at 100%.

Sizing above 100% can make sense if you expect to add an electric vehicle or heat pump. It adds cost and exports, so check how your utility credits surplus first on the net metering guide, then compare both sizes in the payback calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate how many solar panels I need?

Divide your 12-month electricity use by your area’s yearly production per kW to get system size, then divide the size in watts by one panel’s wattage and round up. For 12,000 kWh in Texas: 12,000 ÷ 1,513 = 7.93 kW, and 7,930 ÷ 400 = 20 panels. Your installer’s design will account for shading and roof direction.

What is the 120% rule for solar panels?

It limits how much solar can feed into a home’s main electrical panel. Multiply the busbar rating by 1.2 and subtract the main breaker rating; the result is the largest solar breaker allowed. A 200-amp busbar with a 200-amp main breaker leaves 40 amps. A system above that limit may need a panel upgrade or another connection method.

How much electricity does a 400W solar panel produce?

The 400 W figure is a power rating, not a yearly energy total. Over a year, multiply 0.4 kW by your area’s kWh per kW: about 605 kWh in Texas (1,513 × 0.4), 722 kWh in Arizona and 442 kWh in Washington, using PVGIS estimates at the state capitals with 14% system losses.

How many solar panels are needed to power a 2,000 square foot house?

It depends on the home’s electricity use, not its floor area. A home using the 2022 U.S. average of 10,791 kWh a year would need about 18 panels of 400 W in Texas, 17 in California and 25 in Washington. Enter your own yearly kWh for a closer count.
