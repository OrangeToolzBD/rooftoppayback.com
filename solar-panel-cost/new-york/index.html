Solar Panel Cost in New York

In New York, a 7 kW home solar system costs about $19,320 before incentives at $2.76 per watt (EnergySage, September 2026). At 29.49¢ per kWh (EIA, June 2026), it pays for itself in about 7.4 years.

What solar incentives are available in New York?

New York stacks several incentives. The Solar Energy System Equipment Credit is a state income tax credit of 25% of qualified costs, up to $5,000, for your principal residence, claimed on Form IT-255, with unused credit carried forward for five years (NYS Department of Taxation and Finance; page last updated 2019, checked September 16, 2026).

Residential solar equipment is exempt from the 4% state sales tax and the 3/8% MCTD tax; local sales tax is exempt only where the locality has chosen to exempt it (NYS Tax Bulletin TB-ST-776, updated May 18, 2026). Under Real Property Tax Law section 487, systems built before January 1, 2030 can get a 15-year property tax exemption unless your locality has opted out (NYS assessor manual, RPTL 487).

NYSERDA's NY-Sun rebates are paid in blocks that close as they fill. In Con Edison territory, the last residential block, at $0.15 per watt, closed May 29, 2025 (NYSERDA Con Edison dashboard). We could not confirm the current upstate block, so ask your installer. Income-qualified households may get an Affordable Solar incentive, listed at $0.80 per watt upstate and in Con Edison territory and $0.40 on Long Island (NYSERDA dashboard, checked September 16, 2026).

The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit no longer helps here: the IRS says it "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025" (IRS, checked September 16, 2026).

How does net metering work in New York?

New York homes still get net metering, with one added cost. Residential systems connected on or after January 1, 2022 keep Phase One net metering compensation, or can opt into the Value Stack, for a 20-year term. Credits carry over from month to month, and credit left at the end of the term is forfeited (Orange and Rockland net metering FAQ, checked September 16, 2026).

The added cost is the Customer Benefit Contribution (CBC), a monthly charge based on your system's DC size for systems connected since January 1, 2022. Rates change every year and are not locked in. The 2026 residential net-metering rates per kW per month are:

  • Central Hudson: $1.67
  • Con Edison: $1.29
  • LIPA: $1.13
  • National Grid: $0.97
  • NYSEG: $1.19
  • Orange and Rockland: $1.00
  • RG&E: $1.31

Source: utility-published 2026 CBC rates compiled by NYSERDA (checked September 16, 2026).

For a 7 kW system on NYSEG, the charge works out to about $100 a year. That is small next to the savings, but it is a real line on the bill, and it can change. Our net metering guide covers how credits are applied.

Is solar worth it in New York?

New York ranks 5 of 51 in our payback model. A 7 kW system at $2.76 per watt costs an estimated $19,320, produces about 8,862 kWh a year at Albany and saves about $2,613 in year one at 29.49¢ per kWh. Simple payback is 7.4 years with no incentives, flat prices and full retail credit.

Incentives change that a lot. The state credit on this example would be 25% of $19,320, or $4,830, just under the $5,000 cap. Taking it off the cost gives about 5.5 years. Subtracting a NYSEG-level CBC of about $100 a year lengthens that to about 5.8 years. An income-qualified household that also received $0.80 per watt ($5,600 on 7 kW) would be near 3.4 years on the same assumptions, before the CBC.

Net metering at retail makes the base assumption closer to reality here than in states with low export rates, but the credit is only useful if you can use it within the 20-year term.

These remain estimates. The tax credit is only worth its full value if you have enough state tax to use it within the carryforward period, NY-Sun blocks close, and prices differ between Con Edison, upstate and Long Island. Check your case with the solar payback calculator.
