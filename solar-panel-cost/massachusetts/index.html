Solar Panel Cost in Massachusetts

In Massachusetts, a 7 kW home solar system costs about $20,370 before incentives at $2.91 per watt (EnergySage, September 2026). At 29.61¢ per kWh (EIA, June 2026), it pays for itself in about 7.2 years.

What solar incentives are available in Massachusetts?

Massachusetts combines a small tax credit with a production payment. The state residential renewable energy credit is 15% of the net cost of a solar system at your residence or $1,000, whichever is less (Massachusetts FY27 tax expenditure budget, item 1.601; checked September 16, 2026).

The SMART 3.0 program pays for the solar energy your system produces. According to the Massachusetts Clean Energy Center, the 2026 base rate is $0.03 per kWh, low-income customers get $0.06 per kWh, most home systems of 25 kW or less qualify, payments can last up to 20 years, and 2026 applications opened January 1, 2026 (MassCEC, June 10, 2026).

Solar equipment used to power a principal residence is exempt from sales tax (FY27 tax expenditure budget, item 3.405). The Mass Solar Loan program is closed to new applications (MassCEC).

The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit no longer helps here: the IRS says it "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025" (IRS, checked September 16, 2026). With that gone, the state credit and SMART payments are the main statewide help for a 2026 purchase.

How does net metering work in Massachusetts?

Massachusetts still offers net metering at close to retail value for home systems. National Grid's tariff, effective March 1, 2026, treats systems of 60 kW or less as Class I and exempts systems of 25 kW AC or less from the net metering cap (National Grid, M.D.P.U. No. 1625, checked September 16, 2026).

Under that tariff, the credit for each excess kWh equals the sum of the basic service, distribution, transmission and transition charges. It leaves out a handful of smaller charges, including the energy efficiency, renewable energy, SMART and EV charges and the net metering surcharge. So each exported kWh earns slightly less than the full price you pay. Solar systems receive this credit for 25 years, and any unused credit balance carries forward from one billing period to the next.

If National Grid is not your utility, read your own utility's net metering tariff before you size a system. Some older guides still quote a 10 kW single-phase cap exemption, but the 2026 National Grid tariff uses the 25 kW AC threshold.

Because credits carry forward, summer surplus can offset winter bills. Our net metering guide explains how this appears on a bill.

Is solar worth it in Massachusetts?

Massachusetts ranks 4 of 51 in our model. A 7 kW system at $2.91 per watt costs an estimated $20,370, produces about 9,611 kWh a year at Boston and saves about $2,846 in year one at 29.61¢ per kWh. The simple payback is 7.2 years with no incentives and flat prices.

Incentives shorten that. At the 2026 SMART base rate of $0.03 per kWh, the same output would earn about $288 a year, which brings payback to roughly 6.5 years. Taking the $1,000 state credit off the cost as well gives about 6.2 years. A low-income household at the $0.06 rate would do a little better again. These figures assume the SMART payment applies to all of the system's output.

Net metering credits here sit a little below the full retail price because some small charges are excluded, so the full-credit assumption slightly overstates the value of exported power. For a home that uses most of its output, the difference is small.

These are estimates. Prices differ from one utility to another, and your roof, shading and quote will not match the Boston model exactly. Enter your own numbers in the solar payback calculator.
