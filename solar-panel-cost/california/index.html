Solar Panel Cost in California

In California, a 7 kW home solar system costs about $17,640 before incentives at $2.52 per watt (EnergySage, September 2026). At 34.74¢ per kWh (EIA, June 2026), it pays for itself in about 4.4 years.

What solar incentives are available in California?

California's biggest statewide break for home solar in 2026 is a property tax exclusion: adding solar does not raise your assessed value. The law behind it, Revenue and Taxation Code section 73, stays in effect only until January 1, 2027 (California Legislative Information, checked September 16, 2026).

A 2025 bill, SB 710, kept that January 1, 2027 end date rather than extending it, according to the Board of Equalization's analysis. Systems that qualify before the deadline keep the exclusion until the property changes hands (California State Board of Equalization, SB 710 analysis). If this matters to you, confirm timing with your county assessor.

Income-qualified homeowners may be able to use the Disadvantaged Communities Single-family Solar Homes program (DAC-SASH), which the CPUC lists at $3 per watt for homeowners eligible for CARE or FERA, run by GRID Alternatives with $10 million a year through 2030 (CPUC, checked September 16, 2026). Affordable multifamily buildings have a separate program, SOMAH (SOMAH program site).

We did not find a statewide income tax credit or cash rebate for market-rate home solar. The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit no longer helps here: the IRS says it "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025" (IRS, checked September 16, 2026). Local utilities and cities sometimes run their own programs, so check yours too.

How does net metering work in California?

New California solar customers are on the Net Billing Tariff, often called NEM 3.0, not the older net metering. The CPUC says customers applying for interconnection since April 15, 2023 take service on it (CPUC net energy metering and net billing page, checked September 16, 2026).

Under net billing, the power you use yourself still offsets your bill at the full retail price. Power you export is credited at values from the CPUC's Avoided Cost Calculator, which vary by hour and are generally well below retail. The CPUC says the tariff is guaranteed for nine years, and PG&E and SCE residential customers who apply before the end of 2027 get slightly higher export credits for those nine years. Surplus left at the end of the year is paid out at roughly 2 to 3 cents per kWh.

Customers already on NEM 2.0 keep that tariff for 20 years, so a home bought with an existing system may carry different rules. Ask for the interconnection date.

Because exports are worth less than retail, a battery that shifts daytime solar into the evening, or a system sized close to your own daytime use, usually gets more value per kWh. Our net metering guide walks through how export credits appear on a bill.

Is solar worth it in California?

By our calculation, California has the second-fastest solar payback in the country. A 7 kW system at $2.52 per watt costs an estimated $17,640 and saves about $4,012 in year one at the June 2026 average price of 34.74¢ per kWh, for a simple payback of 4.4 years, ranked 2 of 51.

That headline assumes every solar kWh is worth the full retail price, which is not how net billing works. The real answer depends on how much of your output you use at home. If your solar averaged 75% of retail value, payback would be about 5.9 years. At 50% it would be about 8.8 years, and if only a quarter of the value survived it would be about 17.6 years. A home that uses most of its own power, or adds storage, stays near the low end.

Incentives can shorten the estimate for those who qualify, such as DAC-SASH, and the property tax exclusion avoids a higher tax bill if your system qualifies before January 1, 2027. Neither is included above.

Treat these as estimates. Production is modelled at Sacramento, and coastal, desert and mountain homes differ. Utility rates vary widely by territory. Try your own bill and an export value in the solar payback calculator.
