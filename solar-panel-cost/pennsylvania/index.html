Solar Panel Cost in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, a 7 kW home solar system costs about $18,060 before incentives at $2.58 per watt (EnergySage, September 2026). At 21.73¢ per kWh (EIA, June 2026), it pays for itself in about 9.0 years.

What solar incentives are available in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania has few direct incentives for home solar in 2026. The state's solar resource hub for residents, run by the Department of Environmental Protection, does not list a state rebate or state tax credit for home systems (PA DEP solar resource hub, checked September 16, 2026). Its main support is net metering, covered below.

Sales tax is not waived. A Department of Revenue bulletin says residential solar systems do not qualify for the sales tax exemption used by manufacturers, even if some of the electricity is sold back (PA Department of Revenue, Sales and Use Tax Bulletin 2010-01). Expect sales tax to be part of an installed price unless your installer shows otherwise.

Pennsylvania also has a market for Solar Renewable Energy Certificates (SRECs), but prices are set by trading, not by the state. We could not confirm a current 2026 price from an official source, so we do not include one. If an installer counts SREC income, ask where the price comes from and whether it is guaranteed.

We also could not confirm a statewide property tax exemption from an official page.

The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit no longer helps here: the IRS says it "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025" (IRS, checked September 16, 2026). With no federal credit and no state rebate, the savings on your bill carry most of the payback in Pennsylvania.

How does net metering work in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania offers full retail net metering for home systems. The Department of Environmental Protection says residential systems up to 50 kW can net meter, with excess generation credited at the full retail rate (PA DEP, checked September 16, 2026).

The year-end treatment is where the value drops. In PPL's tariff, extra kWh carry forward at full retail value until the end of the PJM planning year on May 31. Whatever remains is then paid at PPL's Price to Compare, the default generation price, rather than the full retail rate. Customers who buy power from a competitive supplier get the terms of their supplier contract instead (PPL Electric Utilities net metering tariff, Supplement 275; checked September 16, 2026).

Other utilities publish their own net metering tariffs, so check your utility's version for the details.

Two practical points follow. First, a system sized to your yearly use keeps nearly all of its value, because surplus from sunny months offsets winter bills. Second, if you shop for electricity, read the supplier contract for how it handles solar credits before switching. Our net metering guide explains the basics.

Is solar worth it in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania ranks 12 of 51 in our model. A 7 kW system at $2.58 per watt costs an estimated $18,060, produces about 9,198 kWh a year at Harrisburg and saves about $1,999 in year one at the June 2026 average price of 21.73¢ per kWh. Simple payback is 9.0 years with no incentives and flat prices.

Because Pennsylvania credits exports at retail during the year, the full-credit assumption behind that figure is reasonable for a system sized to your use. If a larger system left more surplus at the May 31 true-up, that surplus would be worth the lower Price to Compare, and payback would lengthen. For example, if the system's output averaged 75% of retail value, payback would be about 12.0 years.

With no state rebate or tax credit to subtract, SREC income is the main possible addition. Since we cannot source a current price, it is not included. Add it yourself in the calculator's incentive field only if you have a firm figure.

These are estimates. Prices vary between PECO, PPL, Duquesne and other utilities, and between standard service and supplier contracts. Western and northern sites may produce less than the Harrisburg model. Test your own numbers in the solar payback calculator.
