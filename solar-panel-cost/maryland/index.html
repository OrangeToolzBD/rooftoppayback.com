Solar Panel Cost in Maryland

In Maryland, a 7 kW home solar system costs about $18,200 before incentives at $2.60 per watt (EnergySage, September 2026). At 21.84¢ per kWh (EIA, June 2026), it pays for itself in about 8.4 years.

What solar incentives are available in Maryland?

Maryland's old Residential Clean Energy Rebate is closed; its last solar round we found ended November 30, 2024 (Maryland Energy Administration FY25 FAQ). Its successor, the Maryland Solar Access Program, is open for income-eligible households: the fiscal 2027 portal opened July 29, 2026, and reservations close May 31, 2027 (Maryland Energy Administration, checked September 16, 2026). It pays $750 per kW DC, up to $7,500 per household (Office of the Governor, July 29, 2026). Income limits depend on household size, for example $136,785 for one person.

Solar Renewable Energy Certificates (SRECs) can add income. Under the Brighter Tomorrow Act, certified SRECs from systems placed in service between July 1, 2024 and January 1, 2028 count at 150% of their value for 15 years (Maryland Code, PUA §7-709.1; PSC fact sheet, April 2026). The price itself is set by the market.

Solar energy equipment is exempt from sales tax (Tax-General §11-230), and solar energy property is generally not subject to real property tax (Tax-Property §7-242). All were checked September 16, 2026.

The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit no longer helps here: the IRS says it "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025" (IRS, checked September 16, 2026).

How does net metering work in Maryland?

Maryland offers net metering for systems up to 2 MW, under a statewide program cap of 3,000 MW. Excess generation accrues as credit, either for up to 12 months or indefinitely, depending on the option you choose. With the 12-month option, leftover credit is paid out around the end of April at the average commodity rate of the prior 12 months, which is lower than the full retail price (Maryland Code, Public Utilities §7-306, checked September 16, 2026).

The cap is the thing to watch. A 2026 fiscal note on House Bill 1476 cites the Public Service Commission's expectation that the 3,000 MW cap would be reached in about two years. The bill would have raised the cap to 6,000 MW and required a successor-program report, but its legislative record shows it stopped after a March 3 hearing (Department of Legislative Services, HB 1476 fiscal note; HB 1476 bill page). So the 3,000 MW cap still stands, and rules for customers after the cap have not been set.

If you are planning a system, it is worth asking your utility how close the program is to the cap and whether joining now locks in current terms. Sizing close to your annual use keeps most output at retail value. See our net metering guide for how credits work.

Is solar worth it in Maryland?

Maryland ranks 11 of 51 in our model. A 7 kW system at $2.60 per watt costs an estimated $18,200, produces about 9,905 kWh a year at Annapolis and saves about $2,163 in year one at 21.84¢ per kWh. The simple payback is 8.4 years with no incentives and flat prices.

For an income-eligible household, the Solar Access Program changes the picture. At $750 per kW, a 7 kW system would get $5,250. Subtracting that brings payback to about 6.0 years on the same assumptions. Households above the income limits do not get this, so the 8.4-year figure is their starting point.

SREC income could shorten payback further, especially with the 150% multiplier for systems placed in service before January 1, 2028, but we do not include a price because the market sets it. Net metering at retail supports the full-credit assumption while the program is under its cap. A future successor program could value exports differently.

These are estimates. Prices differ between BGE, Pepco, Delmarva and other utilities, and your roof, shading and installer quote will differ from our model. Enter your own figures in the solar payback calculator, including any rebate you have confirmed.
