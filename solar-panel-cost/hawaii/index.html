Solar Panel Cost in Hawaii

In Hawaii, a 7 kW home solar system costs about $24,010 before incentives at $3.43 per watt (EnergySage, September 2026). At 52.72¢ per kWh (EIA, June 2026), it pays for itself in about 3.9 years.

What solar incentives are available in Hawaii?

Hawaii's main solar incentive in 2026 is the state renewable energy income tax credit. For a single-family home it is worth 35% of the system's cost or $5,000 per system, whichever is less, and each 5 kW counts as one system (Hawaii Department of Taxation, Form N-342 instructions, rev. 2025; checked September 16, 2026).

That credit is changing. Act 24, signed May 21, 2026, adds a $40 million statewide yearly cap on the credit from 2027 and sets an end date, which the State Energy Office puts in 2030. An executive order lets systems placed in service in 2026 avoid the cap if the owner relied on the credit before May 21, 2026 (Hawaii State Energy Office, June 12, 2026). Details of the new eligibility and certification rules had not been published when we checked (Energy Office tax credit page, September 16, 2026).

For batteries, Hawaiian Electric's Battery Bonus closed to new participants on July 1, 2024 (Hawaiian Electric). Its Bring Your Own Device Plus program is open and pays $400 per kW of battery capacity you commit, with an extra $400 per kW for low-to-moderate income customers, under a five-year agreement (Hawaiian Electric, checked September 16, 2026).

The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit no longer helps here: the IRS says it "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025" (IRS, checked September 16, 2026).

How does net metering work in Hawaii?

Hawaii no longer offers classic net metering to new customers. Hawaiian Electric closed its older export programs, including Customer Grid-Supply, Smart Export and Customer Self-Supply, to new customers on March 29, 2024, and its Smart DER tariff began on April 1, 2024 (Hawaiian Electric customer choice page, checked September 16, 2026).

Under the current Smart Renewable Energy Export option, the power you send to the grid earns a credit that depends on the time of day, not your full retail price. The rates published for Oahu are 18.9¢ per kWh overnight, 13.5¢ in the daytime and 32.9¢ in the evening peak, and each island has its own set. The credit rates are locked for seven years, and any credits left over expire at the annual true-up (Hawaiian Electric Smart Renewable Energy Export page, checked September 16, 2026). Confirm the rate in force for your island before you sign, because the page may lag behind rate updates.

The practical effect: daytime exports earn far less than the 52.72¢ average retail price in our table, while solar you use yourself, or store in a battery and use in the evening, keeps its full value. So the share of output you use on site drives the value of a Hawaii system. Our net metering guide explains the difference between net metering and net billing.

Is solar worth it in Hawaii?

On our numbers, Hawaii is the fastest-payback state in the country. A 7 kW system at $3.43 per watt costs an estimated $24,010 and saves about $6,167 in its first year, for a simple payback of 3.9 years, ranked 1 of 51. That assumes no incentives, flat prices and every solar kWh worth the full retail price.

Two things move that figure in opposite directions. The state tax credit would help: taking a $5,000 credit off the cost brings the estimate to about 3.1 years with the same assumptions. Export rules push the other way. Under Smart Renewable Energy Export, exported power earns less than retail, so the real figure depends on how much you use on site. If your solar averaged half the retail value, the no-incentive payback would stretch to about 7.8 years; at 75% it is about 5.2 years.

These are estimates, not quotes. Production is modelled at Honolulu, prices are the state average for June 2026, and your roof, shading, island rate and installer price can all differ. The tax credit also depends on the new 2027 cap and your own tax situation. Enter your own figures in the solar payback calculator, including the export value, to see your own range.
