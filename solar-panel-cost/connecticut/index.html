Solar Panel Cost in Connecticut

In Connecticut, a 7 kW home solar system costs about $18,690 before incentives at $2.67 per watt (EnergySage, September 2026). At 24.32¢ per kWh (EIA, June 2026), it pays for itself in about 8.3 years.

What solar incentives are available in Connecticut?

Connecticut's home solar incentive is built into how you are paid for your power, through the Residential Renewable Energy Solutions (RRES) program, described below. For batteries, the Energy Storage Solutions program changed on April 1, 2026: it now pays an upfront $30 per kWh for standard projects or $130 per kWh for grid-edge projects, plus performance payments for years 1 to 10 of $300, $450 or $550 per kW for standard, underserved and low-income customers (Energy Storage Solutions, checked September 16, 2026).

Two tax breaks apply. Sales and use of solar electricity generating systems are exempt from sales tax (Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-412). Residential solar systems installed on or after October 1, 2007 can be exempt from property tax if they do not produce more than the home uses; you apply to your town assessor by November 1 (Conn. Gen. Stat. §12-81). Both were checked September 16, 2026.

RRES replaced both legacy net metering and the earlier Residential Solar Investment Program when it started in 2022 (PURA).

The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit no longer helps here: the IRS says it "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025" (IRS, checked September 16, 2026). Enrollment in RRES itself ends after 2027, so timing matters for anyone weighing a Connecticut system.

How does net metering work in Connecticut?

Connecticut ended traditional net metering for new customers. It was replaced by RRES, a six-year program that began in January 2022, according to the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA, checked September 16, 2026). State law makes 2027 the sixth and final enrollment year, for systems of 25 kW or less, with terms of up to 20 years (Conn. Gen. Stat. §16-244z).

You choose one of two options, as PURA's FAQ describes them (PURA RRES FAQ, January 2024):

  • Buy-all: the utility buys all the energy and renewable energy certificates your system produces at a fixed price for 20 years.
  • Netting: the solar you use offsets your bill, the utility buys the certificates, and exports earn on-bill credits at the retail rate that carry over month to month.

PURA says it approved updated rates for 2026, but its page does not list them and we could not confirm the figures on Eversource or UI's official pages. Get the current buy-all and netting rates from your utility or installer in writing before comparing. Our net metering guide explains netting in general.

Is solar worth it in Connecticut?

Connecticut ranks 9 of 51 in our model. A 7 kW system at $2.67 per watt costs an estimated $18,690, produces about 9,261 kWh a year at Hartford and saves about $2,252 in year one at 24.32¢ per kWh. The simple payback is 8.3 years with no incentives and flat prices.

That figure fits the netting option best, since the solar you use and your exports are both valued near retail. Netting customers also sell their renewable energy certificates to the utility, which is not in our number. Under buy-all, your savings come from the fixed payment rate instead of your bill, so the answer depends on that rate. We did not include a 2026 rate because we could not confirm it officially. For comparison, if your solar averaged 75% of retail value, payback would be about 11.1 years.

The bigger timing question is that RRES enrollment ends after 2027. What replaces it has not been set in the sources we checked, so a later system may face different terms.

These are estimates. Eversource and UI prices differ, and your roof may not match the Hartford model. Put your quote and the rate your utility confirms into the solar payback calculator.
