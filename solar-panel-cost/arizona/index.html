Solar Panel Cost in Arizona

In Arizona, a 7 kW home solar system costs about $15,960 before incentives at $2.28 per watt (EnergySage, September 2026). At 15.18¢ per kWh (EIA, June 2026), it pays for itself in about 8.3 years.

What solar incentives are available in Arizona?

Arizona offers a state income tax credit for home solar: 25% of the cost of the device, up to $1,000 per residence. Credit you cannot use in one year can be carried forward for up to five years (Arizona Revised Statutes §43-1083, checked September 16, 2026). The state tax form page did not load for us, so check the current form with the Arizona Department of Revenue when you file.

Two tax breaks also apply. Sales of solar energy devices can be deducted from the state sales tax base, provided the retailer is registered with the Department of Revenue as a solar energy retailer (A.R.S. §42-5061). For property tax, the law says solar energy devices are considered to add no value to the property, so a system should not raise your assessment (A.R.S. §42-11054). Both were checked September 16, 2026.

We did not find a statewide cash rebate for home solar in 2026. Some utilities and cities run their own programs, and their terms change, so ask your utility directly.

The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit no longer helps here: the IRS says it "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025" (IRS, checked September 16, 2026). That removes the largest discount Arizona buyers used to count on, which makes the state credit and the export rate below more important to the math.

How does net metering work in Arizona?

Arizona's large utilities do not credit exports at the retail price. Instead they pay a Resource Comparison Proxy (RCP) export rate, and whatever solar you use at home simply reduces the power you buy.

At APS, the rate for customers in the 2026 tranche, running September 1, 2026 to August 31, 2027, is $0.05554 per kWh. Earlier tranches were $0.06171 (2025) and $0.06857 (2024). The rider says the RCP rate cannot drop more than 10% a year, and the rate you start with applies to you for 10 years (APS Rate Rider RCP, Decision No. 82122, checked September 16, 2026). So joining sooner can lock a higher export rate, although the difference between tranches is a few tenths of a cent.

Tucson Electric Power, UniSource and SRP set their own export rates, and we could not confirm their current 2026 figures from their official pages. If you are in one of those territories, get the rate from the utility's current rate sheet before you rely on a quote.

Compared with the 15.18¢ average retail price in our table, an APS export earns roughly a third as much. Shifting use into sunny hours, or adding a battery, keeps more of your output at full value. See our net metering guide for how export rates show up on a bill.

Is solar worth it in Arizona?

Arizona ranks 10 of 51 for payback in our model. A 7 kW system at $2.28 per watt costs an estimated $15,960, produces about 12,635 kWh a year thanks to strong sun at Phoenix, and saves about $1,918 in year one at 15.18¢ per kWh. That gives a simple payback of 8.3 years with no incentives and flat prices.

The catch is the assumption that every kWh saves the full retail price. With the APS export rate at 5.554¢, a home that exports half its output would get about 68% of full value on average, and payback would stretch to roughly 12.2 years. The $1,000 state credit trims that to about 11.4 years. A household that uses most of its solar directly, for example running air conditioning during the day, lands closer to the 8.3-year figure.

Your own rate plan also matters, because the statewide average price in our table blends APS, TEP, SRP and smaller utilities, and your plan may price daytime and evening power differently. These figures are estimates, not a promise of savings.
