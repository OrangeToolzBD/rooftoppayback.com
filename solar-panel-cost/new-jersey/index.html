Solar Panel Cost in New Jersey

In New Jersey, a 7 kW home solar system costs about $18,130 before incentives at $2.59 per watt (EnergySage, September 2026). At 24.95¢ per kWh (EIA, June 2026), it pays for itself in about 7.6 years.

What solar incentives are available in New Jersey?

New Jersey's main incentive is a production payment: the Administratively Determined Incentive (ADI), paid as SREC-IIs for each megawatt-hour your system generates. The Board of Public Utilities cut the residential net-metered value from $85 to $77 per MWh for registrations received on or after July 27, 2026, with a 15-year incentive term (NJ BPU order, May 21, 2026).

Registration is first come, first served, and stays open until the residential block fills or June 1, 2027, whichever comes first (New Jersey Clean Energy Program, checked September 16, 2026). The BPU order sets the residential block for energy year 2027 at 300 MW.

Two tax breaks apply. Solar energy devices, including storage, are exempt from sales tax, using Form ST-4 (NJ Division of Taxation, Bulletin S&U-6). For property tax, a limited exemption covers the increase in assessed value from a certified system; you file Form CRES with your assessor after the local enforcing agency certifies it (NJ Division of Taxation, property tax abatements page). Both were checked September 16, 2026.

The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit no longer helps here: the IRS says it "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025" (IRS, checked September 16, 2026).

How does net metering work in New Jersey?

New Jersey homes are still on net metering: excess solar generation is banked as credit against later bills rather than paid at a lower export rate each month.

Using Jersey Central Power & Light as an example, extra kWh roll forward through the annual period. At the end of that period, any banked credits still left are paid at the avoided cost of wholesale power (FirstEnergy / JCP&L New Jersey net metering page, checked September 16, 2026). That wholesale price is much lower than the retail price, so a system sized well above your yearly use earns little on the surplus.

We did not find an official page showing a 2026 change to the state's net metering rules. Each utility publishes its own net metering page, so check yours for its annual period and how the payout is made.

Net metering is separate from the ADI incentive. Net metering gives you bill credit for the kWh you export, while SREC-IIs pay you for every MWh the system generates, whether you use it or export it. Sizing the system close to your annual use keeps nearly every kWh at retail value. Our net metering guide covers the basics.

Is solar worth it in New Jersey?

New Jersey ranks 6 of 51 in our model. A 7 kW system at $2.59 per watt costs an estimated $18,130, produces about 9,590 kWh a year at Trenton and saves about $2,393 in year one at 24.95¢ per kWh. The simple payback is 7.6 years, with no incentives and flat prices.

The ADI payment is not in that figure. If every one of those 9.59 MWh earned an SREC-II at the new $77 value, that would add about $738 a year for the 15-year term. Adding it to the bill savings would bring payback to roughly 5.8 years on the same assumptions. The exact amount depends on registration timing, metered output and program rules.

Net metering keeps the retail assumption realistic for a system sized to your use, since excess credits carry forward and only the year-end leftover drops to wholesale value. Oversizing weakens that.

These are estimates. Utility prices differ across PSE&G, JCP&L and Atlantic City Electric territory, and your roof may produce more or less than the Trenton model.
