Solar Panel Cost in Washington

In Washington, a 7 kW home solar system costs about $17,710 before incentives at $2.53 per watt (EnergySage, September 2026). At 14.91¢ per kWh (EIA, June 2026), it pays for itself in about 15.3 years.

What solar incentives are available in Washington?

Washington's main solar incentive in 2026 is a sales tax exemption. For systems up to 100 kW AC, which covers home installations, the law exempts 100% of state and local sales tax, taken at the point of sale with an exemption certificate (RCW 82.08.962, checked September 16, 2026).

The exemption has an end date. The Department of Revenue says it expires January 1, 2030 (Washington Department of Revenue, checked September 16, 2026). Ask your installer to show the exemption on the quote rather than adding sales tax.

The state's production incentive, the Renewable Energy System Incentive Program run by Washington State University, is no longer an option for new systems. WSU says the program is fully subscribed, with final payments being processed in 2026 (WSU Energy Program, checked September 16, 2026).

We did not find a statewide cash rebate or state income tax credit for home solar in the sources we checked. Utility programs change often, so ask your own utility what it offers.

The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit no longer helps here: the IRS says it "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025" (IRS, checked September 16, 2026). With no federal credit and no state production payment, the sales tax exemption is the main statewide help left for a 2026 purchase.

How does net metering work in Washington?

Washington law requires utilities to offer net metering until June 30, 2029, or until installed net-metered capacity reaches 4% of the utility's 1996 peak demand, whichever comes first. After that, a utility may adopt a successor tariff, while customers already connected keep their existing terms, as the statute sets out (RCW 80.60.020, checked September 16, 2026).

This matters now because the Department of Commerce says several utilities have already reached that threshold, without naming them in the page we read. Commerce is also running a value-of-solar study, with its final report due October 1, 2026 (Washington Department of Commerce, checked September 16, 2026). That report could shape what successor tariffs look like.

In practice, check two things with your utility before you sign: whether it still offers retail-rate net metering to new customers, and if not, what it pays for exported power. We could not confirm the current status at every Washington utility from their official pages.

If your utility offers full net metering, excess credits offset later bills. If it has moved to a lower export rate, the value of your system drops. Our net metering guide explains both setups.

Is solar worth it in Washington?

Washington is one of the slower payback states in our model, ranked 46 of 51. A 7 kW system at $2.53 per watt costs an estimated $17,710, produces about 7,742 kWh a year at Olympia and saves about $1,154 in year one at 14.91¢ per kWh. That is a simple payback of 15.3 years with no incentives, flat prices and full retail credit for every kWh.

The reason is the combination of cloudy-sky production, at 1,106 kWh per kW, and electricity prices below the national leaders. If your utility has moved beyond full net metering and exports end up worth, say, 75% of retail on average, payback would stretch to about 20.5 years, which is near the end of many panel warranties.

The sales tax exemption helps only if the quote you are comparing includes sales tax; we cannot say whether the installed prices behind our table include it. Production is modelled at Olympia and prices are a statewide average, so a sunnier site or a higher utility rate than average would shorten the estimate.

These are estimates. Solar here can still make sense for reasons beyond payback, such as backup power with a battery, but the savings case is thinner than in most states. Run your own bill, roof and export rate through the solar payback calculator.
