Solar Panel Cost in Maine

In Maine, a 7 kW home solar system costs about $20,580 before incentives at $2.94 per watt (EnergySage, September 2026). At 29.59¢ per kWh (EIA, June 2026), it pays for itself in about 7.6 years.

What solar incentives are available in Maine?

Maine has no state cash rebate for home solar in 2026. Efficiency Maine says it is not currently offering rebates for solar projects (Efficiency Maine, checked September 16, 2026).

The main statewide help is a property tax exemption. For assessments made on or after April 1, 2025, qualifying solar equipment is exempt, including panels, racking, inverters, wiring and batteries that store the panels' output. Most home arrays qualify, and if you buy a house that already has solar, you file an updated application with the local assessor (Maine Revenue Services, Property Tax Bulletin No. 29, December 30, 2024).

If you add a battery, Efficiency Maine's Small Battery Incentives pay $160 per kW per year for letting the battery support the grid during peak events. The payment is passed to you through participating battery manufacturers; it does not cover the purchase price (Efficiency Maine small battery incentives).

We did not find a state sales tax exemption for home solar in the sources we checked. DSIRE lists Maine's net energy billing program, covered below (DSIRE, updated March 3, 2026).

The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit no longer helps here: the IRS says it "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025" (IRS, checked September 16, 2026). That leaves bill savings and the property tax exemption as the core of the Maine case.

How does net metering work in Maine?

Maine uses net energy billing. Homeowners join the kWh credit program, which is open to all customers for systems under 5 MW. Unused credits expire after 12 months (Maine Public Utilities Commission, checked September 16, 2026).

DSIRE describes the kWh credit program as working like traditional net metering: production and use are netted each month, and any extra carries forward as kWh credits. Credits still unused at the end of a rolling 12-month period cannot be used (DSIRE, net energy billing, updated March 3, 2026). A separate tariff-rate program pays a dollar credit, but it is for non-residential customers.

Maine changed net energy billing in 2025 with LD 1777, signed June 27, 2025. The Office of the Public Advocate says the law maintains the rooftop solar program without changes, while reducing payments for community solar (Maine Office of the Public Advocate). DSIRE notes that a successor program is planned.

The 12-month expiry is the key design point. Summer surplus can cover winter use, but credits older than a year are lost, so sizing the system near your yearly use matters. Our net metering guide covers how credits work.

Is solar worth it in Maine?

Maine ranks 7 of 51 in our model. A 7 kW system at $2.94 per watt costs an estimated $20,580, produces about 9,149 kWh a year at Augusta and saves about $2,707 in year one at 29.59¢ per kWh. The simple payback is 7.6 years with no incentives and flat prices.

Maine's high electricity price does most of the work here, and net energy billing credits kWh one for one within the 12-month window, so the full-credit assumption is reasonable for a system sized to your use. A system that regularly produced more than you use would lose the expired credits. For example, if only three-quarters of the output ended up offsetting your bills, payback would be about 10.1 years.

There is no rebate to subtract. The property tax exemption helps by keeping your assessment from rising, which our number does not count. A battery enrolled in Efficiency Maine's program would earn its $160 per kW each year, but also adds cost.

These are estimates. Prices differ by utility, and northern or shaded sites may produce less than the Augusta model. A future successor program could also change credit rules. Check your figures in the solar payback calculator.
