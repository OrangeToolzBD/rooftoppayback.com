Solar Panel Cost in Rhode Island

In Rhode Island, a 7 kW home solar system costs about $19,390 before incentives at $2.77 per watt (EnergySage, September 2026). At 29.23¢ per kWh (EIA, June 2026), it pays for itself in about 7.0 years.

What solar incentives are available in Rhode Island?

Rhode Island offers one of the larger state solar grants. The Renewable Energy Fund small-scale solar grant, run by Rhode Island Commerce, pays $1.65 per watt on systems up to 8.8 kW, to a maximum of $14,500 per project, for systems you own directly, plus $5,000 when storage is added (RI Commerce program flyer, revised April 3, 2026). Grants are only for net-metered projects (REF small-scale RFP).

Funding comes in rounds. The 2026 schedule lists rounds dated May 1, July 17 and October 16, 2026, and each block closes on its application close date or when funds run out (Rhode Island Commerce, checked September 16, 2026).

An alternative is the Renewable Energy Growth program for systems of 25 kW DC or less, which pays 31.55¢ or 28.65¢ per kWh depending on system size and a 15- or 20-year term. The 2026 program year offers 3 MW DC, first come, first served, through March 31, 2027 (Rhode Island Energy, 2026 small-scale solar REG program).

Solar panels are exempt from sales tax (R.I. Gen. Laws §44-18-30), and residential systems in service after December 31, 2015 are exempt from property tax (R.I. Gen. Laws §44-3-3). The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit no longer helps here: the IRS says it "is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025" (IRS, checked September 16, 2026).

How does net metering work in Rhode Island?

Rhode Island's net metering law credits the solar power you produce, up to 100% of your own use, at the sum of the standard offer, distribution, transmission and transition charges, which is close to your full price per kWh. Output above 100% and up to 125% of your use is credited at the wholesale electricity rate (R.I. Gen. Laws §39-26.4-2; §39-26.4-3; checked September 16, 2026).

The statute caps net-metered systems at 10 MW, far above any home system, and for residential systems of 25 kW or less the utility may carry credits forward month to month. In practice, size the system to your own yearly use: anything beyond that earns only the wholesale rate.

Rules may change. A local news report from August 2026 describes a pending request by Rhode Island Energy to the Public Utilities Commission to change solar credit rules; no decision had been reported at the time (Uprise RI, August 2026). Check the current status before you sign.

If you choose the Renewable Energy Growth program instead, you are paid a fixed rate per kWh rather than netting against your bill, so compare both with your installer. Our net metering guide explains the difference between the two approaches.

Is solar worth it in Rhode Island?

Rhode Island ranks 3 of 51 in our model. A 7 kW system at $2.77 per watt costs an estimated $19,390, produces about 9,527 kWh a year at Providence and saves about $2,785 in year one at 29.23¢ per kWh. The simple payback is 7.0 years with no incentives and flat prices.

The state grant can change that dramatically. A 7 kW system is under the 8.8 kW limit, so at $1.65 per watt the grant would be $11,550. Subtracting it brings payback to about 2.8 years on the same assumptions. The grant is paid in funded rounds, so it is not guaranteed for every applicant.

Net metering near retail value supports the full-credit assumption for a system sized to your use. A system producing more than your use would earn only the wholesale rate on the extra, which lengthens payback, and any change to credit rules could also shift the picture.
