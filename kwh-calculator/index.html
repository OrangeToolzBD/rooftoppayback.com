kWh Calculator

This kWh calculator converts power and time into energy: kWh = watts × hours ÷ 1,000. A 1,500 W appliance running for 3 hours uses 4.5 kWh. It also works in reverse, turning kWh over a number of hours back into average watts, and converts battery mAh or amp-hours to kWh at a given voltage.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Rooftop Payback

How do you convert watts to kWh?

Multiply watts by hours, then divide by 1,000. The division converts watt-hours to kilowatt-hours, the unit on your electricity bill.

  • 1,500 W × 3 hours = 4,500 Wh = 4.5 kWh.
  • 60 W × 10 hours = 600 Wh = 0.6 kWh.
  • 3,000 W × 0.5 hours = 1,500 Wh = 1.5 kWh.

To go the other way, divide kWh by hours and multiply by 1,000. Using 4.5 kWh over 3 hours is an average draw of 1,500 W. Choose “kWh → watts” in the calculator for this.

To price the energy, multiply kWh by your rate. At Texas’s June 2026 average of 15.94¢ (EIA), the 4.5 kWh above costs about 72¢. The electricity cost calculator does this for a whole list of appliances by the month.

For larger amounts, 1 megawatt-hour (MWh) is 1,000 kWh, so divide kWh by 1,000 to get MWh.

How many kWh is a mAh battery?

Multiply the capacity in mAh by the voltage to get milliwatt-hours, then divide by 1,000 for watt-hours and again by 1,000 for kWh. A 5,000 mAh battery at 3.7 V holds 5,000 ÷ 1,000 × 3.7 = 18.5 Wh, which is 0.0185 kWh.

Voltage is essential. Amp-hours alone do not measure energy, because the same Ah rating stores four times as much at 48 V as at 12 V. Use the nominal voltage printed on the battery.

The same math sizes home storage. A 12.35 kWh solar battery bank is about 257 Ah at 48 V (kWh × 1,000 ÷ 48). The solar battery calculator works out that capacity from your daily loads, depth of discharge and efficiency.

The result is stored energy, not usable energy. Conversion losses and the share of capacity a battery can safely use mean you get back less than the label.

What is the difference between kW and kWh?

A kilowatt (kW) measures power, the rate of energy use at a moment. A kilowatt-hour (kWh) measures energy, the total used over time. A 1 kW load running for one hour uses 1 kWh; running for 5 hours, it uses 5 kWh.

Solar systems use both units. System size is rated in kW, while production is counted in kWh. A 7 kW system in Texas is modelled to produce about 10,591 kWh a year (7 × 1,513 kWh per kW, from PVGIS at Austin).

Generators and inverters are rated in watts or kW because they must meet peak demand at any instant. Batteries and bills are measured in kWh because they track energy over time. Size a backup unit with the generator size calculator and estimate yearly solar output with the solar panel calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Is 70 kWh per day a lot?

Yes, compared with the average home. EIA reports 10,791 kWh a year for the average U.S. residential customer in 2022, about 29.6 kWh a day, and 14,774 kWh in Louisiana, the highest state, about 40.5 kWh a day (EIA). Seventy kWh a day is more than twice the national figure.

How many kWh is one unit of electricity?

One unit on an electricity bill is one kilowatt-hour. It is the energy a 1,000 W appliance uses in one hour, or a 100 W appliance in ten hours. “Unit” is simply another name for a kWh on some bills, so a bill showing 300 units means 300 kWh were used.

How much does 1 kWh per hour cost?

Using 1 kWh every hour is a steady 1 kW draw. Over a day that is 24 kWh, which costs about $3.83 at Texas’s June 2026 average of 15.94¢, or about $4.40 at the U.S. average of 18.34¢. Multiply by your own rate for your figure.
