Is There a Solar Tax Credit in 2026?

There is no federal solar tax credit in 2026 for new home systems. The IRS says of the Residential Clean Energy Credit: “The credit is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025” (IRS, reviewed July 4, 2026). Systems finished in 2025 can still be claimed.

Is the federal solar tax credit still available in 2026?

No. The Residential Clean Energy Credit, which covered 30% of the cost of home solar panels and, from 2023, battery storage, does not apply to property placed in service after December 31, 2025 (IRS). The IRS’s own timeline lists the 30% rate for 2022 to 2025 only (IRS home energy tax credits, reviewed July 8, 2026).

The change came from Public Law 119-21, enacted July 4, 2025. In its fact sheet FS-2025-05 (August 21, 2025), the IRS says the credit “will not be allowed for any expenditures made after December 31, 2025” (IRS FAQ).

Older pages may still describe the credit as lasting for years to come. They reflect the rule before the 2025 law. For a system installed in 2026, plan on no federal credit and check state and local programs instead.

What if my system was installed in 2025?

If the installation was completed in 2025, the credit can still apply, and you claim it on your 2025 tax return. The IRS says you must claim it “for the tax year when the property is installed, not merely purchased” (IRS).

The date that counts is when installation was completed, not when you signed or paid. The IRS FAQ says that if installation is completed after December 31, 2025, the expenditure is treated as made after that date, and the credit is not allowed (IRS FS-2025-05). A deposit paid in 2025 does not qualify a system finished in 2026.

The credit is claimed on Form 5695, Residential Energy Credits; the latest revision listed by the IRS is for 2025 (IRS, About Form 5695). If the credit is larger than your tax, the IRS says you can carry the unused amount forward to reduce tax in future years. Keep your contract, invoices and any document showing the completion date. For your own return, consider asking a tax professional.

Which incentives still apply in 2026?

State tax credits, state sales tax exemptions, performance payments and utility programs can still apply, and they vary by location. A good starting point is DSIRE, the incentive database run by the N.C. Clean Energy Technology Center at N.C. State University, which you can search by state or ZIP code.

Three examples, each checked on the program’s own official page:

  • New York: a state income tax credit equal to 25% of qualified solar equipment spending, capped at $5,000, for systems at your principal residence (NY Department of Taxation and Finance). The state’s 2025 instructions for Form IT-255 still describe it, with unused credit carried forward up to five years (IT-255-I).
  • Washington: a sales and use tax exemption on equipment for solar systems from 1 kW to 100 kW AC, purchased between July 1, 2019 and December 31, 2029 (Washington DOR special notice).
  • New Jersey: SREC-II payments through the ADI program; net-metered residential registrations received on or after July 27, 2026 earn $77 per SREC-II (NJ Clean Energy Program).

Program rules and funding change, so confirm each one before you sign. The solar incentives guide explains how each type works.

How does losing the credit change payback?

Without the 30% credit, the same system takes longer to pay for itself. For a 7 kW system in Texas at state-average inputs, payback is 9.3 years with no credit. Had 30% of the $15,750 cost come off, the net cost would have been $11,025 and payback about 6.5 years.

High electricity prices still carry payback in some states. California comes out at 4.4 years and New York at 7.4 years without any incentive. In New York, the state credit would be 25% of $19,320, or $4,830, for a state-average 7 kW system, if you qualify. Entered as an incentive, that shortens payback from 7.4 to about 5.5 years.

These figures hold electricity prices flat and value every solar kWh at the retail price. Use the solar payback calculator with your own price, quote and any state incentive to see your number, or compare every state on the cost by state table.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a solar tax credit for 2026?

Not at the federal level for new home systems. The IRS says the Residential Clean Energy Credit is not available for property placed in service after December 31, 2025 (IRS). Some states offer their own credits: New York’s is 25% of qualified costs, up to $5,000. Search DSIRE for your state, then confirm on the program’s own page.

Can I still get an IRS tax credit for solar panels?

Only if installation was completed by December 31, 2025. In that case you claim the credit on Form 5695 with your 2025 return, and any amount larger than your tax can be carried forward. Paying for a system in 2025 is not enough on its own; the IRS looks at when installation was completed.

Will the New York State solar tax credit be available in 2026?

The New York tax department’s credit page states no end date, and its current Form IT-255 instructions, for tax year 2025, still describe the 25% credit capped at $5,000 (NY IT-255-I). The system must be at your principal residence in New York. Check the 2026 form when the department releases it.

Will the federal solar tax credit come back?

Only Congress can change it, and no forecast is offered here. Under current law, as the IRS describes it, the residential credit ends for property placed in service after 2025. This page will be updated if the IRS guidance changes, and the payback tools assume no federal credit until it does.
