Methodology

This page sets out each calculator’s formula as the code applies it, and the source and date behind every default. All calculations run in your browser. Every default can be replaced with your own figure, and results are estimates rather than quotes.

Data sources and refresh schedule

  • Electricity price (¢ per kWh, residential, by state): U.S. Energy Information Administration, Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.A. Current figures are for June 2026, from the release of August 26, 2026, fetched September 16, 2026. Refreshed monthly after each release.
  • Installed cost per watt (by state): EnergySage cost of solar panels state pages, “as of September 2026,” fetched September 16, 2026. The national figure, $2.60 per watt, is from EnergySage’s page updated June 30, 2026, and is used for Alaska, which has no state figure. Refreshed monthly. If a state page’s wording changes, the value is left blank rather than guessed, and the national figure is used with a note.
  • Production per kW (kWh a year) and best tilt and direction: European Commission Joint Research Centre, PVGIS 5.2 (PVcalc), NSRDB radiation data, 1 kWp crystalline silicon, 14% system losses, optimized slope and azimuth, fixed mount, at each state capital. Fetched September 16, 2026. Refreshed yearly.
  • Sun position: NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, General Solar Position Calculations.
  • Federal credit: IRS Residential Clean Energy Credit page, checked September 16, 2026: “The credit is not available for any property placed in service after December 31, 2025.” Incentive inputs therefore start at $0. State and utility incentives are researched from DSIRE and each program’s own page, and dated.

Solar payback calculator

Gross cost = system kW × 1,000 × cost per watt. Net cost = gross cost − incentives, not below zero. For each year n: savings = kW × kWh per kW × (1 − degradation)^(n−1) × price × (1 + price increase)^(n−1) × export value %. Savings accumulate year by year; payback is the year the total reaches the net cost, interpolated within that year. If that does not happen within 40 years, the result says so. The 25-year savings and net gain (savings − net cost) are also shown. Defaults: state averages above, export value 100%, price increase 0%, degradation 0%, incentives $0.

The state table on the cost-by-state page applies the same formula to a 7 kW system with those defaults, and ranks states by payback.

Solar panel cost calculator

Size = the kW you enter, or, if you enter yearly use, yearly kWh ÷ the state’s kWh per kW. Cost before incentives = size × 1,000 × the state’s cost per watt (national figure if none).

Solar panel calculator

Yearly use = your figure × 12 if entered per month. System kW = yearly use × share to cover ÷ state kWh per kW. Panels = system watts ÷ panel watts, rounded up. Cost = system kW × 1,000 × cost per watt.

Solar loan calculator

Monthly payment = amount × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where r = APR ÷ 12 and n = years × 12; if the APR is 0, payment = amount ÷ n. Total interest = payment × n − amount. Monthly savings = system kW × state kWh per kW × state price ÷ 12, for year one with flat prices and full retail value. The result shows savings minus payment.

Solar battery size calculator

Capacity (kWh) = daily kWh × days ÷ usable depth of discharge ÷ round-trip efficiency. Amp-hours = capacity × 1,000 ÷ bank voltage (12, 24 or 48 V).

Solar panel angle calculator

Best fixed tilt and direction are the PVGIS optimum for the state capital. Sun position follows the NOAA equations: fractional year γ = 2π ÷ 365 (366 in leap years) × (day of year − 1 + (hour − 12) ÷ 24); the equation of time and declination are computed from γ; solar noon = 720 − 4 × longitude − equation of time + 60 × time zone, in minutes of local standard time; the noon elevation is 90° minus the zenith angle at that time. Time zones are standard time; daylight saving time is not applied.

Electricity cost calculator

For each appliance, monthly kWh = watts × hours per day × days per month ÷ 1,000. Monthly cost = total kWh × price, using your price if entered or the state’s EIA average. Yearly cost = monthly cost × 12.

kWh calculator

kWh = watts × hours ÷ 1,000. Average watts = kWh × 1,000 ÷ hours. Wh = mAh ÷ 1,000 × volts, and kWh = Wh ÷ 1,000.

Generator size calculator

Running load = the sum of running watts. Surge = the largest single (starting − running) difference, since motors are assumed to start one at a time. Peak = running load + surge. Size to look for = peak × (1 + safety margin). The example appliance values are placeholders, not measurements.

Testing and updates

Each calculator has automated tests whose expected values were computed independently, including a NOAA check for Denver on June 21, 2026. When a data source is refreshed, the affected pages are rebuilt and their updated date changes only if their numbers changed.
